# Source comparison for the sparse critical boundary

Inspected on 2026-10-13 UTC. This is a bounded source/overlap check, not a
certificate of historical firstness.

* Bartłomiej Bychawski, arXiv:2406.06267v1 (submitted 2024-06-10):
  <https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06267v1>. The live arXiv abstract page exposes
  only v1. The primary paper defines full TF pairs for all graphs, defines
  its projection group on reduced graphs, and states the uniform labelled
  expected-size question as Conjecture 6.6. The inspected Section 6 does
  not formulate the sparse boundary (1) of `CRITICAL_BOUNDARY.md`.
* MathDB public submitted solution, attributed on the inspected page to
  Shivam Patel: <https://mathdb.com/p/363470/conjecture-on-the-expected-size-of-the-two-fold-automorphism>.
  Its displayed proof addresses the fixed-p full-group limit and then the
  projection/reduced interpretations. Its component argument retains degree
  and equality-rank losses; it does not contain the `4 log n-4` boundary,
  the logarithmic-square correction 8, or the cycle/excess split inspected
  here. Its independent acceptance status is not inferred from its posting.

The fixed-p limit, full-group definition, equality-component probability
method, and classical permutation cycle counting are prior mathematical
input. The new note isolates its contribution as the quantitative sparse
boundary and the all-support summation using component endpoint invariance.

Two search systems were queried with combinations of “two-fold
automorphisms”, “random”, “expectation”, “sparse”, “critical”, and “4 log”.
Returned nearby material included fixed-p group questions, random-graph
canonization, and graph-lifting articles. No matching proof of this precise
sparse correction was found in the inspected primary material. Coverage
was limited: several search results were unrelated, and a guessed v2 HTML
endpoint failed; v1 was subsequently opened through the actual abstract
page link. Neither that failed endpoint nor a search absence establishes
that no prior solution exists.
