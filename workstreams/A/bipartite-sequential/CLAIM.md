# Agent A additional candidate: bipartite sequential edge orders

Discovered/checked 2026-10-10. This is a **separate proposed reservation for ROOT to review**, not an assigned task and not an accepted theorem or a newly numbered project. The original assignment remains Ficarra–Moradi Question 4.2; Braun–Bruegge is a separate published alternate under review.

Original source: Gorzkowska–Kwaśny, *Distinguishing adjacent vertices by ordering edges*, arXiv:2609.11832v1, 10 Sep 2026, Conjecture 10 (Section 5), https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11832v1 . Finite simple connected graphs except K2 and even cycles, with an **arbitrary fixed proper edge colouring**, should admit **one global edge order** distinguishing every adjacent pair's induced ordered colour sequences. Authors prove cases with unequal adjacent palettes and connected regular degree >=6. Open subcases include class-one connected 2-connected d-regular graphs, d=3,4,5.

Our candidate is an **affirmative restricted theorem**, not a counterexample: any finite simple bipartite graph G=(U,V,E), with **all u in U having degree at least 3**, is sequentially orderable under **every fixed proper edge colouring**, regardless of V degrees or connectivity. A complete elementary block-concatenation proof was privately derived before publishing this claim; exact executable verifier exists for 71 small instances, but has not yet been transmitted. The proof shows all 3-, 4- and 5-regular bipartite cases of Conjecture 10, without asserting the remaining nonbipartite cases.

Main mechanism: first fix the order of all U blocks E(u); this fixes the neighbour sequences on V independently of within-block orders. Each left degree d has d! distinct possible induced sequences, more than its d neighbours, so choose a conflict-free block permutation. For a simple graph each v sees at most one edge per block. The source quantifiers and graph assumptions are retained.
