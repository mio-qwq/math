## Precise statement

Let `G` be a finite simple undirected graph admitting a TF automorphism `(alpha, alpha^{-1})`, with `alpha` of **odd** order. Form a quotient graph `Q` on its **moving alpha-orbits** (length > 1); two orbit vertices of `Q` are adjacent when at least one graph edge joins them.

**Bundle lemma.** Let `O_0,...,O_{k-1}` be any **simple odd cycle** in `Q`, with `k>=3` odd. Let `m_j=|O_j|`, each an odd integer >=3, and `M=min_j m_j`. Then `G` contains **pairwise vertex-disjoint simple cycles**

\[
C_k \quad\text{and}\quad (M-1)/2\text{ copies of }C_{2k}.
\]

All these cycles lie among the selected `k` orbits and together use **exactly M distinct vertices from each orbit**. If all `m_j=M`, their union is a spanning 2-factor of the subgraph on the chosen orbit vertices (other edges may exist).

**Corollary, conditional on the earlier group/quotient proof:** Every finite asymmetric unstable graph has a nontrivial TF automorphism `(alpha,alpha^{-1})` of odd order and a nonbipartite moving-orbit quotient. It therefore contains the above bundle for at least one odd `k`. This strengthens the **second** conjecture clause of Russell Mizzi, [arXiv:2603.27559v3 §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27559v3#S7), which asks only for `C_k` and `C_{2k}` in every unstable asymmetric graph, without asserting disjointness.

The separate first clause involving **two different TF-cousin graphs** is **not** settled here.

## Complete proof of the bundle lemma

Label each `O_j` by `Z/m_j Z`, so that `alpha(x)=x+1`. For each successive orbit pair `O_j,O_{j+1}`, choose one **actual** edge with labels `(a_j,b_j)`. The TF transformation takes it, for every integer `t`, to `(a_j+t,b_j-t)`. By the generalized Chinese remainder theorem, this edge orbit consists of exactly **all pairs of labels with sum**

\[
x+y\equiv s_j:=a_j+b_j\pmod{\gcd(m_j,m_{j+1})}.
\]

Let `L=lcm(m_0,...,m_{k-1})` (odd), and choose arbitrary integer representatives `S_j` of these residues. Solve over `Z/L Z`:

\[
z_j+z_{j+1}=S_j \qquad (j\bmod k).
\]

For **odd k**, successive substitution yields

\[
2z_0=S_0-S_1+S_2-\cdots+S_{k-1}\pmod L.
\]

Since `L` is odd, `2` is invertible; the cyclic system has a unique solution. In particular all original edges between `(j,z_j+t)` and `(j+1,z_{j+1}-t)` are present, for **every** integer `t`; the corresponding edges with opposite signs are present too.

The vertices `(j,z_j)` for `j=0,...,k-1` form an actual simple `C_k`. Put `a=(M-1)/2`. For **each** `t=1,...,a`, list

\[
(j\bmod k,\ z_{j\bmod k}+(-1)^j t),\qquad j=0,1,\dots,2k-1 .
\]

Consecutive terms, including the final closing edge, have offset signs `+t,-t` and hence are adjacent by the selected TF edge orbit. Because **k is odd**, the two traversals of the `k` quotient orbits use opposite offset signs; thus this is a **single** simple `C_{2k}`, using two vertices in each selected orbit.

The shift set `{-a,...,-1,0,1,...,a}` has `M` distinct integers and diameter `M-1`. Since `m_j>=M`, its members stay pairwise distinct **modulo every m_j**. The `C_k` and all `a` doubled cycles therefore have no shared vertices. They use precisely the `M` shifts at each orbit, giving `Mk` covered vertices. This proves the lemma. `QED`

The bundle lemma itself **does not require G to be asymmetric or connected**: only the stated odd TF symmetry and a chosen odd cycle in its moving-orbit quotient. Asymmetry enters solely through the earlier independent argument forcing an odd quotient cycle.
