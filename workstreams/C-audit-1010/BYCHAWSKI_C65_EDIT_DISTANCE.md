## Source definition, exact conjecture, stronger target

Bartłomiej Bychawski, *Constructions of graphs with any possible two-fold automorphism and automorphism groups*, arXiv:2406.06267v1 (2024), **Conjecture 6.5**, end of Section 6: For any ε>0, the probability that a uniformly random labelled n-vertex graph can be converted to some graph with nontrivial two-fold projection `Aut^π` in at most n(1−ε)/2 edge/nonedge flips tends to zero. Paper sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06267 and its available full text https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381307706_Constructions_of_graphs_with_any_possible_two-fold_automorphism_and_automorphism_groups . **Definition 1.4** defines `Aut^π` on **reduced** (open-neighbourhood-twin-free) graphs; this issue must not be silently elided. We prove both lower and upper bounds for the more restrictive **connected, nonbipartite, reduced** target class, so the paper's intended interpretation is covered.

Let `G_n ~ G(n,1/2)` (uniform labelled graph) and let `D_n` be the minimum number of undirected edge toggles needed to obtain a **connected, nonbipartite, open-neighbourhood-twin-free** graph `H` with `|Aut^π(H)|>1`. This minimum is finite for n sufficiently large. Ordinary nontrivial graph automorphisms count, as `Aut(H)≤Aut^π(H)` by the paper's Observation 1.5. The strengthened theorem is

\[
\boxed{\frac{D_n}{n}\ \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb P}\ \frac12.}\tag{1}
\]

In particular, for every fixed ε>0,

\[
\Pr\left(D_n\le\frac n2(1-\varepsilon)\right)\longrightarrow0,
\]

**affirmatively answering original Conjecture 6.5** if the proof passes independent review. A matching upper statement is also obtained: for any fixed ε>0, `Pr(D_n<=n(1+ε)/2)->1`. We do not claim a central limit theorem, a sharp second-order fluctuation law, or a guaranteed world-first proof.

## A. Lower bound, uniformly over **all possible** target TF groups

A target `H` in the original reduced class with nontrivial `Aut^π(H)` has a nonidentity first-coordinate projection of some TF pair `(α,β)`. In particular it has some **nonidentity ordered TF pair** satisfying, on all ordered vertex pairs,

\[
A_H(u,v)=A_H(\alpha(u),\beta(v)).\tag{TF}
\]

Write `S=supp α ∪ supp β`, `s=|S|`. The subgroup generated by α,β partitions S into orbits `O_1,...,O_t` of sizes `ℓ_i≥2`; no orbit is a singleton, since each S vertex is moved by at least one of α,β. Let `W=[n]\S`.

### A1. Many **independent comparison pairs** outside S

Fix a vertex w∈W. The original TF equation and its transpose imply every edge variable from w to one Γ-orbit O_i must be equal. Within O_i choose `floor(ℓ_i/2)` **disjoint vertex pairs**, and demand equality of the two edge bits from w to each pair. Over all such choices and all w∈W, these comparisons involve **disjoint, independent** Bernoulli(1/2) random edge bits from the original random graph G_n. Thus the number `Z` of comparisons where the two bits disagree has the exact binomial law

\[
Z\sim\operatorname{Bin}(L,1/2),\quad
L=(n-s)\sum_{i=1}^t\lfloor\ell_i/2\rfloor.
\tag{2}
\]

To turn G_n into **any** H invariant under this fixed TF pair, each disagreeing comparison must have at least one of its two edges toggled, and no chosen edge bit participates in two comparisons. Therefore the **edit distance to this entire TF-invariant family is at least Z**.

Every orbit size ℓ≥2 satisfies `floor(ℓ/2)≥ℓ/3`, so

\[
L\ge\frac{s(n-s)}3.
\tag{3}
\]

For `s=2,3`, there is at least one comparison pair per w, so `L≥n-s`. For `4≤s≤8`, there are at least **two** such pairs per w: a single orbit of size≥4 yields two, while a partition into shorter orbits with no singletons also yields two. Thus `L≥2(n-s)` for this range.

### A2. Supports s=2,3 (the sharp obstruction)

Set `d=floor(n(1−ε)/2)` for any fixed ε∈(0,1). For `s=2` or `s=3`, select **one** comparison pair per w∈W, giving `Z_0~Bin(n-s,1/2)`. Its mean is (n-s)/2. For `n≥2s/ε`, the gap between this mean and d is at least εn/4. Hoeffding's inequality for independent Bernoulli variables gives

\[
\Pr(Z_0\le d)\le \exp(-\varepsilon^2 n/8).
\]

There are at most `binom(n,s)(s!)²≤n^{2s}` choices of a TF pair with union support s. For s=2,3 their total probability tends to zero.

### A3. Supports 4≤s≤8

Select exactly **two** of the independent comparison pairs per w, giving `Z_0~Bin(2(n-s),1/2)`. Its mean is `n-s`, while `d≤n/2`. For `n≥32`, the gap is at least `n/4`. Hoeffding yields

\[
\Pr(Z_0\le d)\le\exp(-n/16).
\]

The at-most-polynomial `Σ_{s=4}^8 n^{2s}` candidate pairs cannot overcome this exponentially small probability.

### A4. Supports 9≤s≤n/4

By (3) and `n-s≥3n/4`, we have `L≥sn/4≥(9/4)n`. Hence `d≤n/2≤L/4`. A multiplicative Chernoff/Hoeffding bound gives

\[
\Pr\bigl(\operatorname{Bin}(L,1/2)\le d\bigr)
\le\Pr(\operatorname{Bin}(L,1/2)\le L/4)
\le e^{-L/8}\le e^{-sn/32}.
\]

Summing over at most `n^{2s}` ordered TF pairs of each such support gives

\[
\sum_{s=9}^{\lfloor n/4\rfloor}
\exp\{s(2\log n-n/32)\}\longrightarrow0,
\]

because the negative n/32 term eventually dominates `2log n`.

### A5. Supports s>n/4

This range cannot be handled by using only one or two local comparison pairs, because the number of possible TF pairs grows rapidly. Instead use the separately **proved general TF entropy lemma** in [BYCHAWSKI_C66_EFFECTIVE_BOUND.md](BYCHAWSKI_C66_EFFECTIVE_BOUND.md): for every fixed TF pair whose union support exceeds n/4, its allowed symmetric 0–1 graph matrices have at most `2^{E−Δ}` members, where `E=binom(n,2)` and `Δ≥n(n−3)/32`. This follows from the outdegree≤1 functional equality constraint graph, not from sampling.

The number of labelled graphs within `d≤n/2` edge changes of this family is at most

\[
2^{E-\Delta}\sum_{j=0}^d\binom Ej.
\]

For random uniform G_n, this gives probability at most

\[
2^{-\Delta}\sum_{j=0}^d\binom Ej
\le2^{-n(n-3)/32}(d+1)E^d.
\]

There are at most `(n!)²` possible TF permutation pairs. The logarithm base 2 of their union bound is at most

\[
-\frac{n(n-3)}{32}+2n\log_2n+
\log_2(d+1)+d\log_2 E
=-\Omega(n^2),
\]

since `d=O(n)` and `E=O(n²)`. Therefore the dense-support contribution tends to zero.

Combining A2–A5 establishes the **Conjecture 6.5 lower limit**, even if the candidate target family were enlarged to include **any graph with any nonidentity TF pair**, whether or not it satisfied connectedness/reducedness.

## B. Matching **upper bound** inside the source's actual reduced/connected/nonbipartite class

For a uniform random graph G_n, fix two vertices x=0,y=1 and leave **all edges induced by W=[n]\{x,y} unchanged**. Explicitly edit:

1. Make xy an edge (at most one toggle).
2. For each w∈W, make xw equal to the *original* yw bit (toggle exactly when these independent old edge bits disagree).

The result H has x,y adjacent and with the **same neighbours in W**; the transposition τ=(xy) is a genuine ordinary automorphism of H, so `|Aut^π(H)|≥2` whenever H is reduced. The number of toggles is

\[
X_n+I_{xy\notin E},\qquad X_n\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n-2,1/2).
\]

It is at most `n(1+ε)/2` with probability tending to one by Hoeffding or the weak law of large numbers.

Crucially, the edited H is **reduced** with probability tending to one; this is not inferred merely from H's ordinary symmetry. If the unedited induced graph `G_n[W]` is reduced, no pair of vertices in W can become open-neighbourhood twins after the edit, because all their adjacencies **within W remain unchanged**. Nor can x or y be open twins with any other vertex: since xy is an edge and x,y have identical external neighbours, an assumed open-twin relation between x and some w∈W would force simultaneously `xw=0`, `yw=1` (because x is adjacent to y), and `xw=yw`, a contradiction. The same for y. Hence H is reduced whenever `G_n[W]` is reduced.

Furthermore, if `G_n[W]` is connected and nonbipartite, and y has at least one neighbour in W, then H is also connected and nonbipartite. For `G(n−2,1/2)`, connectedness, nonbipartiteness and reducedness each hold with probability tending to one by elementary union bounds, while `Pr(y has no neighbour in W)=2^{-(n-2)}`. These properties can be verified independently from the **unedited** induced W graph; the edit never changes it.

Thus with probability tending to one our explicit H satisfies **every** original target hypothesis — connected, nonbipartite, reduced, nontrivial `Aut^π` — and lies within `n(1+ε)/2` toggles of G_n. This proves the upper half of (1), completing the strengthened sharp-threshold candidate theorem.
