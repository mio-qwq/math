Definitions: G is a finite SIMPLE UNDIRECTED CONNECTED graph. An x-y detour is a longest SIMPLE x-y path (not necessarily induced, not a shortest path). I_D[x,y] is the union of vertices on ALL longest x-y paths. S is detour-irredundant if for every distinct x,y,z in S, z is outside I_D[x,y]. dir(G) is the maximum cardinality of such a set. D=max_{x,y}D(x,y) is the length in EDGES of a globally longest simple path. The claimed bound is dir(G)<=n-D+1. A refutation is a connected graph G, an explicit set S with |S|>n-D+1, and exact exclusion of every selected third vertex from every longest selected-pair path.

Verification plan: construct adjacency from an edge list. Discovery can enumerate simple paths, retaining longest endpoint-pair lengths and the union of their vertex sets, then solve the finite forbidden-triple problem. A candidate will be checked by a separately implemented subset-DP plus a complete written argument where available. A spanning path gives D=n-1 without optimization, so traceable graphs and a three-vertex detour-irredundant set are a first diagnostic mechanism. Merely showing one long path containing three selected vertices does not establish the numerical counterexample.
