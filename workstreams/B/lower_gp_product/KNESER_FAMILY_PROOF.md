# The lower-GP product conjecture for two-subset Kneser factors

B,2026-10-10. **Computer-assisted all-parameter subclass proof; pending independent review.** This is not a solution for arbitrary factors and not a counterexample.

## Result

Let K(n,2) have the two-element subsets of an n-element set as vertices, adjacent precisely when disjoint. For all integers n,m>=5,

    gp^-(K(n,2) □ K(m,2)) >= min{gp^-(K(n,2)),gp^-(K(m,2))}.

Thus the original lower-GP Cartesian-product conjecture holds throughout this two-parameter family. For n,m>=10 a useful explicit version is

    gp^-(K(n,2) □ K(m,2)) >= min{floor(n/2),floor(m/2),6}.

Moreover, if 10<=min(n,m)<=13, the exact value is floor(min(n,m)/2), namely5or6. If both parameters are at least14, only the conjectured lower bound6 is established here; the failed six-point upper construction is not an impossibility theorem.

The original statement is Kruft Welton–Khudairi–Tuite, arXiv2404.19451v1 Conjecture2.10, final2025 Conjecture3; [primary PDF](https://oro.open.ac.uk/98050/9/98050final.pdf). The universal truncation at4 is B's separately frozen ebaa409418b53983ef1bd52abe34703c5dca6618, proof SHA256 cbc5d97fb06da0317e36973ea6e9ef4ec6e70f89cb8799c1f6d4701c014b5075. Its arbitrary-graph proof is used for product sets of size<=3 and for the small-parameter regime.

## 1. Known factor parameters and metric

For n>=5, distances in K(n,2) are0for identical sets,1for disjoint sets and2otherwise: two distinct intersecting pairs use at most three symbols, leaving two symbols for a common neighbor. Thus K(n,2) is connected of diameter2. Alphabet restriction to any at-least-five-symbol subalphabet is isometric.

The known factor formula is gp^-(K(5,2))=4 and, for n>=6,

    gp^-(K(n,2))=min{floor(n/2),6}.

This is prior work: Di Stefano, Klavzar, Krishnakumar, Tuite and Yero, *Lower General Position Sets in Graphs*, arXiv2306.09965v1, Theorem5.1; [author-hosted primary paper](https://users.fmf.uni-lj.si/klavzar/preprints/2306.09965.pdf). We do not count these factor values as new.

A short verification of the large-n ingredient is included to make the dependence transparent. A GP-set in a diameter-two graph induces a disjoint union of cliques, since an induced P3 is exactly a forbidden triple. In K(n,2), a clique is a matching of underlying symbol pairs. A component with at least three edges forces every selected pair into that same matching component: a pair outside it would have to meet three disjoint pairs, impossible with two symbols. A maximal such matching has floor(n/2)members. If there is a two-vertex component, its two disjoint symbol pairs use four symbols and every other component must use cross-pairs on those four symbols; extendibility then forces all six pairs. Finally, an independent pair-family is either a star or contained in a triangle. A star that is not full extends within the star; the triangle extends inside the six pairs of a four-symbol set. Hence the minimum maximal size for n>=6 is min{floor(n/2),6}. The six pairs on four symbols and a full matching attain the relevant alternatives. The isolated Petersen boundary n=5 is also checked directly by verify_kneser_factor.py, in addition to its published value.

## 2. Why two finite checks imply infinitely many cases

Let S be a k-point subset of K(n,2) □ K(m,2). Its first coordinates use at most2k symbols, and the same is true separately for the second coordinates. If n,m>=2k+2, choose in each alphabet a subset of exactly2k+2 symbols containing all coordinates of S. The induced factors are K(2k+2,2), and are isometric by Section1. Their product is also isometric, by additivity of Cartesian distances.

Consequently, if EVERY GP k-set in K(2k+2,2) □ K(2k+2,2) has an extension there, then EVERY GP k-set in K(n,2) □ K(m,2) has an extension for all n,m>=2k+2. An extension inside the smaller isometric product retains all strict triangle inequalities in the full product. This is a bounded-support reduction, not an inference from sampling graph orders.

We apply it at k=4 and k=5. The exact finite assertions are:

- Every four-point GP-set of K(10,2) □ K(10,2) extends.
- Every five-point GP-set of K(12,2) □ K(12,2) extends.

## 3. Complete finite quotient and exact checker

The self-contained verifier is `verify_kneser_products.py`; it imports no discovery code and uses only integer arithmetic and Python standard-library routines. The finite verification below is part of this computer-assisted proof.

For a k-point product set, order its k vertices. One coordinate projection is an ordered list of k unordered symbol pairs, with repetitions allowed. All such lists, up to symbol renaming, are generated by set partitions of the2k endpoint positions. Restricted-growth strings represent all set partitions uniquely; adjacent positions belonging to the same pair must have different labels, excluding loops. Sorting each pair and renaming labels preserves all graph relations. Hence this generates every ordered projection, including all repeated-coordinate cases, with harmless duplication only.

For each projection the checker constructs the ORIGINAL Kneser adjacency, computes all factor BFS distances, and retains:

1. the k-by-k distance matrix between its ordered landmarks;
2. the set of all distance vectors from graph vertices to those landmarks, with one actual vertex representative for each vector.

These data suffice exactly: product distances between landmarks add, and a proposed product extension has the sum of its two factor distance vectors. Such a point is already selected exactly when one sum coordinate is zero. Otherwise it extends a GP-set precisely when, for every old pair i,j, its distances t_i,t_j satisfy

    t_i+t_j != d(s_i,s_j), and |t_i-t_j| != d(s_i,s_j).

Thus equality of these retained data preserves GP and extension questions, without relying on an unproved graph-isomorphism assertion. Multiplicities of a distance vector are unnecessary: a zero coordinate uniquely specifies its landmark, and otherwise any realizing vertex suffices.

A projection consisting of k DISTINCT GP points may be omitted: the factor lowerGP-number is k+1 in both tested cases (5for K(10,2),6for K(12,2)), so it extends in that factor. Its extension paired with any opposite coordinate then extends the product set by strict coordinate triangle inequalities.

Finally, the checker takes all simultaneous permutations of the k product indices to reduce the FIRST projection to one orbit representative. The SECOND projection remains unrestricted. This loses no case because reordering the product points acts on both projections together, and the enumerated second-projection family is closed under every index permutation; that closure is asserted during the actual run.

For every remaining GP configuration the checker finds an explicit pair of factor-vertex representatives and checks the resulting (k+1)-point set again by direct triangle equalities using factor BFS distances. It also runs an original Cartesian-product BFS from one root and checks the additive distance bridge at every product vertex. The general additive-distance fact is separately proved in the frozen truncated-bound manuscript.

## 4. Deduction of the original inequality

If at least one parameter lies between5and9, its factor lowerGP-number is at most4, so the universal truncated bound proves the original inequality.

If both n,m>=10, the truncated bound excludes maximal GP-sets of size<=3. Section2 and the k4 finite check exclude size4, so the product lowerGP-number is at least5. This proves the conjecture if at least one factor parameter is10or11.

If both n,m>=12, the k5 finite check also excludes size5, giving product lowerGP-number at least6. Both factor lowerGP-numbers equal6, proving the remaining cases. This proves the entire stated n,m>=5 family.

## 5. Exact subfamilies from the known matching upper bound

A matching of r=floor(n/2)>=3 symbol pairs in K(n,2) is a clique and a terminal set. Indeed every nonselected pair e intersects a matching pair f (at most one symbol is unmatched), and there is another matching pair g disjoint from e. Since f and g are disjoint, e,g,f is a shortest path containing two matching vertices with e as endpoint.

Place this matching in one fixed layer of a Cartesian product. A point outside the layer can follow a shortest path to that layer and then the same two matching vertices; if its first coordinate is already a matching vertex, use that vertex and another matching vertex. Additivity verifies maximality, while the chosen set is a clique and hence GP. This familiar terminal-layer upper bound is already part of the source theory and gives

    gp^-(K(n,2) □ K(m,2)) <= min{floor(n/2),floor(m/2)}  (n,m>=6).

Together with Section4, this gives the claimed exact values when10<=min(n,m)<=13. For both parameters>=14 no exact value is asserted.

## Limitations and reproduction

Run `python workstreams/B/lower_gp_product/verify_kneser_products.py` and `python workstreams/B/lower_gp_product/verify_kneser_factor.py` from the frozen checkout. Actual logs and source hashes are included. The proof is computer-assisted and universal over the stated Kneser family; it is not a universal Lean theorem. The factor formula, matching upper construction, and source definitions are prior results. No historical-firstness, independent-review or arbitrary-factor solution claim is made.

Discovery scripts independently used sequential edge introduction and exhaustive type pairs. Their successful finite outputs alone were not the proof; the separate endpoint-partition generator, original BFS checks, complete quotient argument, and isometric support reduction are essential. The failed attempt to obtain a universal six-point upper bound by permuting two four-symbol six-edge sets is recorded and stopped, not extrapolated.
