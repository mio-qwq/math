A structural obstruction to
(1, 1, 3, 3, 4)-packing colorings

Abstract
Mortada, El Zein and Al Hajjar asked whether every connected claw-free subcubic
graph, apart from their speciﬁed exception, admits a (1,1,3,3,4)-packing coloring. We
give a negative answer. For every odd integer k ≥ 3, the triangle expansion of a ring
of k diamonds is a connected cubic graph on 12k vertices that admits no such coloring.
The proof selects an arbitrary existing high-colored vertex in each triangle. The selected
labels give a proper three-coloring of the core, and the radius-four class forces an external
private neighbor at each of its core vertices. This requirement excludes that class from
every diamond tip, leaving an impossible two-coloring of an odd cycle. The proof is
structural and requires no exhaustive search. A 36-vertex member is also certiﬁed in Lean
using actual graph distances; that formalization does not cover the entire inﬁnite family.
Keywords. Packing coloring; claw-free graph; cubic graph; triangle expansion; counterexam-
ple; formal veriﬁcation.

1 Introduction
A packing coloring assigns diﬀerent distance requirements to its color classes. Repeated
numbers in a prescribed sequence stand for distinct labels, each with its own class. This
distinction is particularly useful for subcubic graphs, where several independent sets and a
few more widely separated classes can be combined. For background on this framework, see
Gastineau and Togni [ 4] and the survey of Brešar, Ferme, Klavžar and Rall [ 3].
Mortada, El Zein and Al Hajjar [ 5] proved that a connected claw-free subcubic graph other
than a speciﬁed graph H admits a (1,1,3,3,3)-packing coloring. They then asked in Section 6,
Problem 1 whether the third radius-three class could always be replaced by a radius-four class.
Their exception H has 12 vertices. Our constructions have at least 36 vertices and satisfy all
the other hypotheses of that question.
The answer is negative even for cubic graphs obtained by the standard operation of replac-
ing each vertex of a cubic core by a triangle. Our cores are odd rings of diamonds. Neither
these cores nor the triangle replacement operation is new: both occur in the structural descrip-
tion of claw-free cubic graphs, for example in Oum [ 7] and Brešar, Kuenzel and Rall [ 2]. The
mathematical content here is the obstruction to the particular distance sequence (1,1,3,3,4)
and its proof for all odd ring lengths.

a vertex with one of the three high labels. Choosing any such vertex gives a proper three-
coloring of the core. For the label of radius four, the chosen incidence must point to a core
