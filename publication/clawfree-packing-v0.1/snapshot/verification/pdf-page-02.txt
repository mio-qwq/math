1.1 Related work and contribution boundaries
Brešar, Kuenzel and Rall [ 2] established (1,1,2,2)-packing colorability for claw-free cubic
graphs. Mortada and El Zein [ 6] subsequently gave a shorter proof. Anderson, Kuenzel and
Marcano Cuellar [ 1] proved (1,1,2,3)-packing colorability for diamond-free claw-free cubic
graphs. The expanded graphs used here are themselves diamond-free: the diamonds lie in
their cores. These positive results concern diﬀerent distance sequences and are consistent with
our obstruction.
The result of Mortada, El Zein and Al Hajjar [ 5, Theorem 4] also implies that every
graph in our family admits a (1,1,3,3,3)-packing coloring. We use that known conclusion
only to place the obstruction in context. We do not claim to refute that theorem, or to
settle their separate (1,1,2,3) problem for general subcubic graphs. We make no claim of
minimum counterexample order, an exact ordinary packing chromatic number, or historical
ﬁrstness. The original arXiv record and related literature were checked in several bounded
searches; absence of an earlier veriﬁed answer in those searches is not an exhaustive priority
certiﬁcation.
2 Deﬁnitions and main result
All graphs in this paper are ﬁnite, simple and undirected. For a graph F , its open neighbor-
hood at v is NF (v). The distance distF (x, y) is the minimum length of an x– y path, with
distance ∞ between vertices in diﬀerent components. Distances in an induced subgraph are
not used for packing constraints. A graph is subcubic if every vertex has degree at most three,
cubic if every vertex has degree three, and claw-free if it contains no induced copy of K1,3.
