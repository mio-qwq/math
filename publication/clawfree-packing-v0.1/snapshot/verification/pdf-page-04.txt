4 A necessary condition on the core
An external private neighbor of v with respect to a set I is a vertex w ∈ ND(v) \I satisfying
ND(w) ∩ I = {v}. We use this standard terminology only for the explicitly stated condition
below; no domination theorem is invoked.
Proposition 4.1. If T (D) admits a (1,1,3,3,4)-packing coloring, then D admits a proper
three-coloring f : V (D) → { A, B, C} such that each vertex of I = f − 1(C) has an external
private neighbor with respect to I.
Proof. Let c be any (1,1,3,3,4)-packing coloring of T (D). All labels have positive radius, so
adjacent vertices have distinct labels. In particular, the triangle Tv cannot be colored solely
with the two labels 1a,1b. Choose a vertex xv ∈ Tv having a high label, and put f (v) = c(xv).
The choice can be arbitrary; Tv may contain more than one high-colored vertex.
If uv ∈ E(D), the triangles Tu, Tv are disjoint, and Lemma 3.2 gives distT (D)(xu, xv) ≤ 3.
Each high label has radius at least three. Therefore f (u) ̸= f (v), and f is proper.
Take v ∈ I and write its selected incidence as xv = ( v, w). Then w ∈ ND(v), and
properness shows w /∈ I. Also v ∈ ND(w) ∩I. If another vertex u ∈ ND(w) ∩I, u ̸= v, existed,
then Lemma 3.2 and ( 3) would give distT (D)(xv, xu) ≤ 4. But these are distinct vertices both
assigned the label C, whose radius is four. The required strict inequality is impossible. Thus
ND(w) ∩ I = {v}, as required.
Remark 4.2. Proposition 4.1 is a necessary condition, not an equivalence or a coloring algo-
rithm. It neither constructs the radius-three representatives nor ensures that the remaining
vertices can be colored by the two low labels. The contradiction below needs only necessity.
In particular, no restriction to colorings with exactly one high vertex per triangle is justiﬁed
or used.
