# Mathematical Research: Proofs, Counterexamples, and Lean Formalization

**English** | [简体中文](README.zh-CN.md)

This repository studies public mathematical problems and provides rigorous counterexamples, complete proofs, structural classifications, and reproducible computational and formalization materials. The work spans graph theory, combinatorics, matrix structures, and homological algebra. Its central aim is to give precise mathematical answers that can be checked independently against the original problems.

We distinguish **mathematical conclusions, formalization coverage, independent review, and publication status**. Written proofs and Lean proofs have different verification scopes; a result on a working branch does not become an accepted main-branch result merely because its author marks it as passed. The accepted-results catalog below reflects the source files and verification records consolidated on **12 October 2026**, with manuscript publication updates of **15 October 2026**; the remote-branch snapshot remains the one checked on **13 October 2026**. Detailed hypotheses, relations to the literature, and subsequent updates are documented in the individual topics. Some linked materials are in Chinese.

| **Main eigenvalues and the canonical double cover (CDC).** Collins–Sciriha, arXiv:1906.05790v1, [Question 5.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.05790), numbered Question 16 in the journal version: do graphs with isomorphic canonical double covers have the same main eigenvalues? | **Complete affirmative answer:** every finite simple graph satisfies $M(\mathrm{CDC}(G))=M(G)$; hence isomorphic double covers imply equality of the sets of main eigenvalues. | [Theorem and attribution](workstreams/ROOT/cdc-main-eigenvalues/THEOREM.md) · [Lean](workstreams/ROOT/cdc-main-eigenvalues/CDCMainEigenvalues.lean) · [Compilation and independent acceptance](workstreams/ROOT/cdc-main-eigenvalues/verification.json) · [Release](https://github.com/mio-qwq/math/releases/tag/cdc-main-eigenvalues-lean-2026-10-10). | **Inheritance of lower general position in Cartesian products.** Kruft Welton–Khudairi–Tuite, [arXiv:2404.19451v1, Conjecture 2.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.19451v1), numbered Conjecture 3 in the journal version: $\mathrm{gp}^-(G\square H)\ge\min\{\mathrm{gp}^-(G),\mathrm{gp}^-(H)\}$. | **Complete counterexample:** factors on 11 and 29 vertices both have lower general position number at least 6, but their 319-vertex product has an inclusion-maximal general position set of only 5 vertices. Here $(x)_j=x(x-1)\cdots(x-j+1)$ denotes a falling factorial. Each entry in a packing-coloring palette represents a **distinct color** with the stated distance radius; vertices of the same color must be at distance strictly greater than that radius. Directed general position requires that no shortest directed path contain three selected vertices. The lower general position number is the smallest cardinality of an **inclusion-maximal**, rather than maximum, general position set.

The circulant-digraph formula uses the paper's remainder convention $n=qd+r$, with $q\ge0$ and $2\le r\le d+1$:

$$
\mathrm{gp}(\mathrm{Circ}(n,\{1,\ldots,d\}))=
\begin{cases}
d+1,&r=d+1,\\
\max\{r,\lfloor d/r\rfloor+1\},&2\le r\le d.
\end{cases}
$$

This is a complete result for consecutive generators, not for arbitrary generating sets. This unnumbered conjecture, the permutation-digraph conjecture in §3.3, and the Kautz special case below are different problems. A proof, a checker, an extension, and a formalization of the same problem are not counted as separate answers to original problems.
